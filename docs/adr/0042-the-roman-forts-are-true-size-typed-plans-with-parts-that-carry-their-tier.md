# ADR 0042: The Roman forts are drawn true size from typed plans whose parts carry their own tier

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Date:** 2026-10-09
- **Decided by:** Claude, under the proactive mandate (ADR 0027), for issue 3, agreed by Dewi on 2026-10-09
  ("built detailed in the app", `process/decision-log.md`); Dewi can overrule any of it.

## Context

The forts were a placeholder: one `roman-fort` feature kind with its own renderer (`romanFort` in
`structures.ts`), two boxes with the wrong position and angle (`roman-fort-a/b`), tiled roofs and a limewashed
headquarters, none of it evidenced at Dinefwr, drawn at the 2.6 times landmark scale. The checked research
(`research/roman-forts-and-vicus.md`) gives measured sizes, ditch counts and profiles, three of Fort 2's four
gates and two of Fort 1's, a six-post gate tower, one interval tower, a vicus of irregular timber buildings,
and says plainly that every interior, roof and tower height is analogy. The issue asks for modular parts,
gates that could open, named and editable objects, levels of detail, and a clear line between documented and
reconstructed. Dewi chose a detailed fort even where it stands out from the low-poly valley, and the research
note left two questions to the builder: how big to draw it, and how detailed to be about what is not known.

## Decision

**One model, not a fort path.** The forts are `building` features with a `Plan`, like the castles and the
town's church; the `roman-fort` kind and `romanFort` renderer are deleted (ADR 0017: architecture is data
plans rendered by one builder). The plan model gained what the forts needed, for every plan to use: a `road`
part; a `ditch` with a `depth` (shaded V) or a `fill` (a backfilled ditch); a `taper` on a wall (a turf bank
narrows); a `roofMaterial` on a tower (a gate tower's roof is timber, not the old hard-coded slate); and a
`PartMeta` on every part: `id`, `name` (both languages), its own `provenance`, and `detail` (ADR 0043). Parts
are generated by small pure helpers in `src/domain/fort.ts` (the rounded ring with its gate gaps, a gate's
frame, a corner's midpoint, roadside houses scattered by a seeded generator) and assembled in
`src/content/forts.ts`, so Fort 1 and Fort 2 share one circuit, one gate and one interior builder.

**Every part carries its tier.** Each part of every fort plan has an id that names it for good
(`fort1/gate-sw/tower-left`), a bilingual name and a provenance of its own, and a content test
(`tests/forts.test.ts`) fails a part without them, a documented part without a source that exists, and a
reconstructed or imagined one without a stated basis in both languages. The key date's card lists them
part by part, each with its note and sources. Gates are separate parts (two towers, a walkway, a passage, two
leaves each), so a leaf can later be built as its own object; the renderer merges them into one mesh per
level today, so no leaf can move yet.

**What is documented and what is not.** Documented: the two outlines and sizes (the inner ditches round 240m by
160m and 140m by 110m), the ditch counts where measured (three south-east and two south-west for Fort 1,
V-shaped, 1.7m by 1.1m inner and 1.0m by 0.5m outer; three or more on Fort 2's south-east side), the evidenced
gateways (Fort 1 south-east and south-west, with a six-post tower at the south-west; Fort 2 front, left, right),
Fort 1's four-post interval tower, the survey's roads, and the vicus's roads. Reconstructed: the turf rampart
(inferred from the ditch fill; its 3m height, 4m base and 1.7m breastwork are Jay Lane analogy), every other
tower, all interiors (the standard auxiliary plan, six barracks at Fort 2 as at Trawscoed), the unmeasured
ditches. Imagined: roofs' material, gate leaves and the vicus's houses. **Fort 2's rear gate is drawn as
unproven** (smaller, unroofed, every part saying "not demonstrated"), and Fort 1's north-east and north-west
gates, which were not found, are not drawn. Neither the annex, the practice camps, the possible bathhouse nor
the kiln is drawn: each is "perhaps" or contested. There is no stone, no tile and no limewash, and a test fails
any. Neil Ludlow's drawing, "for Cambria Archaeology", is credited in each fort's ⓘ and the key date's ⓘ as a
reference, not evidence, and nothing is copied from it.

**True size, true height.** The forts and the vicus are drawn at the map's own horizontal scale and at true
height, a new `Setting` `'true'`: across 1, up 1. The reasons: every number in the evidence is metric (a 1.7m
ditch, 0.4m posts, a 240m side), the plan must register on a real-scale terrain whatever scale its neighbours
are drawn at, and the forts' position is known to about 30m. The 2.6 times landmark scale is for castles that
must read at the overview and whose sites are points; a 240m fort at 2.6 times covers about 26ha, not 3.84, and no
longer sits on its HER point. The `map` setting (the town's) draws true in plan but as tall as the hills are
exaggerated, 2.4 times; with it a 3m barrack became a house and a 3m rampart a 7m wall (seen on the first
screenshot, 2026-10-09), so a place whose heights are evidence gets `true`. The cost is that the forts are
a few triangles across at the overview (the camera's lower limit moved from 25 to 10 units, and the depth of
field's focus floor with it, so the detail can be reached). `true` also hides no modern building: `map` plans
hide the OS footprints under them for good, which no Roman plan should.

**Placement.** Fort 1 about E 262206 N 222470 and Fort 2 about E 262190 N 222538, long axes at bearings 48 and
53 degrees, the research note's re-measured Fig 1 centres (Fort 2 within 5m of Coflein's point, each within 40m
of the HER's), not the placeholders' (Fort 2 had been 28m east and 29m south of Fort 1, both at 7 degrees).
The new LiDAR terrain (issue 2) is not in this branch, so the registration is against the OS Terrain 50 ground
only, and its heights and the ridge's two knolls are unchecked; this is the open question below.

## Consequences

The forts are as editable as any plan: changing a ditch count or a gate is a data edit with a test, the ⓘ
text lives beside the part it describes, and a later LiDAR registration moves `FORT_1_AT`/`FORT_2_AT` and
nothing else. About 65 to 85 parts per fort, which a contributor can read in a diff. Content is held to its
evidence by tests, not review. The `Part` type grows three optional fields that only the forts fill, so other
plans are untouched. The first plan to use `'true'` is also the first whose heights are not exaggerated, so
Newton House and the castles still look different from the forts; that is the 2.6 times convention, not an
inconsistency of the forts.

## Open questions

- Register the forts on the LiDAR when issue 2 lands: the ridge, the saddle and the two knolls should show
  whether the HER-based positions need a nudge (the note puts them at plus or minus 30m).
- Whether the Blender deliverable the issue also asks for is wanted: Dewi chose the in-app build, so the plan
  data is the editable source and no `.blend` exists.

## Alternatives considered

Keeping `roman-fort` as a kind with a richer renderer (a second architecture path with the same provenance
problem); the 2.6 times landmark scale (false by that factor, and unregisterable); the `map` setting (a
tall, house-like fort); a Blender source with glTF (Dewi chose in-app; ADR 0017 keeps architecture procedural);
four towered gates and tidy rows of houses as in the Ludlow drawing (the research marks both as artistic
completion, and the survey shows no grid).
