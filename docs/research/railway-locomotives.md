# The Llanelly Railway's locomotives around 1857

Independently checked 2026-10-02 (see [reviews/railway-locomotives-check-2026-10-02.md](reviews/railway-locomotives-check-2026-10-02.md)); corrections applied.

Research for a model of the train that reached Llandeilo in January 1857 (ADR 0017), written
2026-09-28 from sources fetched that day. The Victorian note left this as a gap ("locomotive and
rolling-stock detail specific to the 1850s–70s on these lines could not be recovered"), and its
suggestion of "0-4-2 or 2-2-2 types" came from general knowledge, not a source.

## Summary

- **Which engine hauled the trains to Llandeilo in 1857 is not recorded in anything read here.**
- **One Llanelly Railway engine of the period is described in detail by a primary source.** Colonel
  Yolland's Board of Trade inquiry into the boiler explosion at Pantyffynnon on 29 January 1858
  describes the engine **Victoria**: "a six-wheeled coupled engine, weighing 18 tons. It had 14½-inch
  cylinders with 16-inch stroke. The wheels were 4 feet in diameter, and 8 feet 6-inch base. It was
  constructed by the Messrs. Hackworth, of Stockton, and delivered in 1841." [S1]
- **Its layout was unusual.** The boiler was 12ft long and 4ft 3in in diameter, with a steam dome over
  its centre. A main tube carried the fire grate at one end and a combustion chamber at the other, and
  68 small tubes led back through the boiler to the smoke-box and chimney: "the furnace, smoke-box, and
  chimney being at one extremity". "The driver having the regulator, steam pressure gauge, water-gauge
  glass, and water cocks, immediately under his control, rode at the leading end of the engine; and
  the fireman, who had to look to the fire and attend to the feed cocks, rode at the other." It ran
  with a tender, which the fireman stood on. [S1]
- **The same company's early engines were Hackworth six-coupled tender engines**, per a secondary
  history of the line: "Victoria" and "Albert", "designed and constructed by T. Hackworth and Company
  complete with outside cylinders, 4ft driving wheels and a full working weight of 14 tons", with "Princess
  Royal" (1842) and the company's own "Prince of Wales" (1843) of the same wheel arrangement. It adds
  that two new engines were delivered in 1857 (not named), and that in 1858 the company took two 0-4-2
  tender engines with outside cylinders from Beyer, Peacock of Manchester, also named Victoria and
  Albert. [S2] The Hackworth builder, six coupled wheels and 4ft wheels are **cross-checked** by [S1];
  the outside cylinders and the rest of [S2] are **single-source**. The 1858 Beyer, Peacock pair is still
  single-source: three searches found no works list, number or second account.
- **Contradiction: when the first engines ran.** [S2] has Victoria and Albert running in 1839; [S1] says
  Victoria was "delivered in 1841"; [S5] (referenced to Denman) has two engines in use from 1840. Shown as
  unresolved. (*Corrected 2026-10-02 (independent check)*: not recorded before.)
- **Contradiction: the weight.** [S1] gives 18 tons; [S2] gives "a full working weight of 14 tons". [S1]
  is the inspector's report on this very engine, written after examining the wreck; [S2] is a 1985 popular
  history giving one figure for two engines. *Corrected 2026-10-02 (independent check)*: the earlier "or one figure may be empty weight"
  is dropped, because [S2] calls its 14 tons the full working weight, so 18 cannot be an empty weight; and
  the repairs [S1] records (12½ cwt of new boiler plate in 1851, 10 cwt in 1857, a new main tube and 18
  stays) come to roughly a ton, not four. Shown as unresolved; the model uses [S1]'s 18 tons.
- **By 1858 Victoria was worn out and "of late mostly employed in ballasting"** (*Corrected 2026-10-02 (independent check)*: the note
  said "used only for ballast trains"; [S1] says "mostly"): it "had been lying by two years and a half,
  having been found too light for the mineral traffic, and never powerful enough for the winter traffic".
  It was repaired from June to December 1857 and was "in substantial repair when taken out of the workshops
  in December 1857", so it was out of service for half of Llandeilo's first railway year. It exploded at
  Pantyffynnon about half-past five, standing beside the passenger platform after bringing down six trucks
  of ballast from the Amman branch, killing three people (*Corrected 2026-10-02 (independent check)*: the note said it exploded "while
  bringing" the trucks; [S2]'s "on the approach to Pantyffynnon station" is contradicted by [S1]). [S1]
- **Later engines are documented but after 1857**: "Napoleon III", a 2-4-0 by Kitson & Co., 1868,
  later GWR 894 [S3][S4] (S3 and S4 are one museum and one photograph, so not independent). **"Victor"**, a
  0-6-0 long-boilered tender engine, Fossick & Hackworth works no. 176, delivered 1864; sold to the
  Carmarthen and Cardigan Railway in December 1872; photographed at Swindon after withdrawal in 1882 [S7];
  scrapped September 1889 [S8] (citing Reed 1956). The 1882 and 1889 dates are withdrawal and scrapping, so
  not necessarily in conflict. So Victor came from the same Stockton firm as Victoria. (*Corrected 2026-10-02 (independent check)*: the
  note said no fetched page confirmed Victor.)
- **The Great Western took over in 1873, not 1889.** The GWR took over the Llanelly company's original
  lines, Llanelli to Llandeilo included, **from 1 January 1873**: "leased to ... GWR, in 1873" [S2]; "the GWR
  took over the LR&DC's original lines from 1 January 1873" [S5]; "taken over by the Great Western Railway on
  1st January 1873 (but not amalgamated until 1st July 1889)" (comment on [S3]). It acquired 21 Llanelly
  engines, renumbered 894 to 914 [S9], which fits Napoleon III becoming 894. The companies amalgamated on
  1 July 1889. So the Llanelly company's own engines are relevant to Llandeilo only for 1857 to 1872.
  (*Corrected 2026-10-02 (independent check)*: added; the app had the Llanelly trains running until 1889.)
- **Context for 1857 to 1872**: Fossick and Hackworth (as Ianson, Fossick and Hackworth) worked the line
  under contract from January 1850 to August 1853 [S5] (ref. MacDermot); from 1867 the company bought six
  standard 0-6-0s with **inside** cylinders, delivered over two years [S2]. So by the late 1860s an
  1841-pattern engine is less typical of what ran than in 1857.

## Other details worth using

- **Railwaymen worked in Welsh.** The driver "told the fireman, in Welsh, to turn the feed-cock". [S1]
  A grounded detail for any railway conversation of the 1850s.
- **A new station was being built at Pantyffynnon junction in January 1858**, and passenger and ballast
  trains shared the line. [S1]
- **The company's fleet was small**: two engines in 1840 and five by 1847 [S5], referenced there to Denman
  (2012), "page needed" (*Corrected 2026-10-02 (independent check)*: the note said unsourced).

## What the model can honestly be

A **reconstructed** Llanelly Railway engine of the period, following the one engine documented in
detail: a six-coupled 18-ton Hackworth engine with 4ft wheels on an 8ft 6in wheelbase, a 12ft by
4ft 3in boiler with a central dome, the fire and chimney at one end, the driver's platform at the
other, and a tender. Outside cylinders rest on [S2] alone. Not recorded, so chosen and labelled: the cab (none is
mentioned, and engines of this date usually had none, which is itself unverified), colours, and the
tender's shape. The ⓘ must say that which engine hauled Llandeilo's trains is unknown.

## Open questions

- The engines actually rostered on Llandeilo passenger trains, 1857–58. Michael Denman, *The Llanelly
  Railway and Dock Company* (2012), has a chapter "The Locomotive Fleet" [S6]; not read.
- Whether Victoria and Albert were Hackworth return-flue engines of the type of *Sans Pareil*; [S1]'s
  description of a main tube with return tubes to a chimney at the fire end suggests so, but it is an
  inference. *Corrected 2026-10-02 (independent check)*: the builder was Thomas Hackworth's firm, "T. Hackworth and Company" [S2], i.e.
  Fossick & Hackworth of Stockton, founded 1839 "by George Fossick and Thomas Hackworth (brother of Timothy
  Hackworth)" [S8]; *Sans Pareil* was Timothy Hackworth's engine, so "his" pointed at the wrong brother.
- Confirm the outside cylinders from a second source; the Beyer, Peacock 0-4-2s of 1858 (works numbers,
  drawings).
- Carriages: what ran to Llandeilo in 1857 is unknown. The secondary history's remark that passengers rode
  "on open passenger carriages or converted goods wagons attached to goods trains" [S2] is dated 1841, so it
  is not evidence for 1857; [S5] agrees for 1841 (a company letter of 14 October 1841, quoted in Denman) and
  adds that passenger trains appeared in Bradshaw in 1850. (*Corrected 2026-10-02 (independent check)*: the note said the remark had no
  date.)
- Some trains through Llandeilo before 1873 may have been the LNWR's, not the Llanelly's: [S5] says the
  LNWR gained running powers and a share of the Vale of Towy lease from about 1868, and [S10] says the
  Central Wales Extension reached Llandovery that year. A lead, not checked.
