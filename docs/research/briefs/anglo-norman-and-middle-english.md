# Brief: Anglo-Norman French and Middle English

Given to a research agent (Claude Opus 5.5) on 2026-10-02. The work was agreed by Dewi: the
design note [`conversations-by-class.md`](../../design/conversations-by-class.md) says a research
pass on both languages is needed before any line is written in them. The task text is summarised
closely below; the output-format boilerplate is abbreviated.

## What was asked

Research what is needed to write short, honest conversations in **Anglo-Norman French** and
**Middle English** for the English garrison at Dinefwr and a magnate's retinue in June 1282, and
more briefly for the Premonstratensian canons at Talley c. 1185 (checking the notes on where they
came from):

1. Which language each group most plausibly spoke in 1282 (garrison soldiers, knights, clerks),
   and how far Middle English and Anglo-Norman were used by the military and administrative
   classes in 1280s Wales and the March.
2. Attested phrases: short, real, citable lines or formulae of the period in either language
   (oaths, greetings, orders, letters of Edward I's officials in Wales in 1282, pay-rolls,
   chronicle speech), with the original, a translation and the exact source.
3. Spelling and grammar guidance for writing plausible new lines in late-13th-century
   Anglo-Norman and in south-western or West Midland Middle English of c. 1280–1300, with model
   sentences from real texts, and what to avoid (modern French, Chaucer's London English).
4. Pronunciation: how a modern `fr-FR` and `en-GB` voice differ from period speech, so the ⓘ can
   say so honestly; whether Middle English read by an `en-GB` voice is intelligible; what
   respelling might help.
5. Names: plausible first names and bynames for English soldiers and Norman knights of the 1280s
   from real records, for imagined characters.

## Method and source priority

Every claim from a source actually read, with what it says. Cross-check important claims against
two independent sources and mark each cross-checked or single-source. Contradictions side by side.
Wikipedia is a lead only. Prefer scholarly editions on archive.org, the Anglo-Norman Dictionary,
the Middle English Dictionary and Compendium, British History Online and peer-reviewed work. At
most 40 WebSearch calls (a shared budget); prefer WebFetch.

## Output

Two files only, no commit: this brief, and the note
[`docs/research/anglo-norman-and-middle-english.md`](../anglo-norman-and-middle-english.md) with the
standard frontmatter, a section per numbered point, open questions, and a numbered Sources list in

unchanged.
