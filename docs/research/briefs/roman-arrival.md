# Brief: the Roman army arrives in the Tywi valley, c. AD 74

Given to a research agent (Claude Opus 5.5) on 2026-10-02. The work was agreed by Dewi as research
for an honest cutscene of the Roman army reaching Llandeilo and Dinefwr. The task text is summarised
closely below; the output-format boilerplate is abbreviated.

## What was asked

Read `CLAUDE.md`, [`../process.md`](../process.md), [`../era-iron-age.md`](../era-iron-age.md),
[`../reviews/era-iron-age-check-2026-10-02.md`](../reviews/era-iron-age-check-2026-10-02.md) and
[`../timeline-and-earliest-occupation.md`](../timeline-and-earliest-occupation.md) first, then research:

1. **The campaign**: who conquered south-west Wales and when (Frontinus, Tacitus on the Silures and on
   Agricola's campaign against the Ordovices, the Demetae's apparent lack of resistance): what the
   sources say and what is inference.
2. **The route**: the Roman roads and forts around the valley (Llandovery and the Alabum
   identification, Carmarthen, the Tywi road, Dolaucothi and Pumsaint, Sarn Helen), with Coflein or
   Archwilio grid references and confidence, and the most plausible direction of approach.
3. **The force**: units attested (if any), legion or auxiliaries, kit, standards, baggage, the
   marching order in Josephus (*Jewish War* III.6.2), and how a marching camp was built. Each detail
   marked documented for this campaign, documented elsewhere, or unknown.
4. **The local people**: the Demetae, their settlements near Llandeilo in AD 74, whether Garn Goch
   was still in use, and what a farmer might have seen.
5. **Season and time**: anything that dates the arrival more closely.
6. **Sounds**: Roman army signals (cornu, tuba, bucina), hobnailed boots, carts.

For each element: what the cutscene could show, and its tier.

## Method and source priority

Every claim from a source actually read; cross-check and mark cross-checked or single-source;
contradictions side by side; Wikipedia only a lead. Prefer Coflein, Archwilio/Heneb, RCAHMW, Roman
Inscriptions of Britain, Lacus Curtius or Perseus for Tacitus, Josephus and Frontinus, and
peer-reviewed work. At most 25 WebSearch calls (a shared budget); prefer fetching known URLs.

## Output

Two files only, no commit: this brief, and the note [`../roman-arrival.md`](../roman-arrival.md) with
the standard frontmatter, a "What the cutscene can show" table (element, what to show, tier,
sources), detail sections, open questions, and a numbered Sources list in the format
`tools/research/extract-sources.mjs` reads. Confirm it parses, then restore the generated file.
