# Brief: music through time and places

Given to a research agent (Claude Opus 5.5) on 2026-10-02. The work was agreed by Dewi as "music
through time and places". The task text is summarised closely below; the output-format boilerplate
is abbreviated.

## What was asked

Research what music was made and heard in and around Llandeilo (the app's 10-mile circle) in each
era, by class and setting, and how the app could play it honestly:

1. **Per era**: Iron Age and Roman (what is known at all, with analogy from elsewhere in Britain
   labelled as analogy); early medieval and the princes of Deheubarth (court bard, pencerdd, harp
   and crwth; the 1176 Cardigan gathering, just outside the area); Talley Abbey's Premonstratensian
   chant; later medieval poets and patrons (Lewys Glyn Cothi, the Rhys family); the Robert ap Huw
   manuscript (c.1613); Tudor and Stuart church music and ballads; Georgian harpers at gentry houses,
   the triple harp, ballad singers at fairs; Victorian chapels and hymn singing, eisteddfodau, choirs,
   brass bands, local musicians and hymn writers, music at the 1857 railway opening and the 1850
   church reopening; the 20th century to today.
2. **Specific pieces**: tunes or hymns with a documented local link, with dates; and period pieces
   with no local link, labelled as such.
3. **How to play it legally**: public-domain notation we could render ourselves, and existing
   recordings with usable licences, checking the licence of both the composition and the recording.
   URL, title, performer, licence as stated, fit. No downloads.
4. Whether reconstructing medieval Welsh harp music from Robert ap Huw is scholarly practice.

A mid-task change from the coordinator (ADR 0026): recordings may also be CC-BY-NC, CC-BY-NC-SA or
CC-BY-SA, as well as CC0, CC-BY, public domain or OGL. Both the composition and the recording must
be open or public domain; "all rights reserved" stays out.

## Method and source priority

Every claim from a source actually read; cross-check and mark cross-checked or single-source;
contradictions side by side; Wikipedia a lead only. At most 30 WebSearch calls (a shared budget);
prefer fetching known URLs. Public-domain primary texts (Brut y Tywysogion 1860, the Ancient Laws
1841, Gerald of Wales, Edward Jones 1794, Mary Morgan 1795) were read from Internet Archive and
Project Gutenberg text layers; Welsh Newspapers Online was read directly.

## Output

Two files only, no commit: this brief, and the note
[`../music-through-time.md`](../music-through-time.md) with the standard frontmatter, a section per
era with a table (music, who, setting, local link, sources), a "Playable pieces and licences"
table, open questions, and a numbered Sources list in the format `tools/research/extract-sources.mjs`
reads. The note is not yet listed in `README.md` or given a prefix in the extractor; that is left
to whoever merges it.
