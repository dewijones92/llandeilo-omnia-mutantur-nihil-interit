# Earliest occupation and key dates timeline (10-mile Llandeilo radius)

## Summary

This document covers everything from the earliest evidence of human presence to today, within a
10-mile (16km) radius of Llandeilo, Carmarthenshire. Research was done in six parallel passes
(deep prehistory; Iron Age/Roman; early medieval/Deheubarth; high medieval castles and the
Edwardian conquest; Tudor–Civil War; Georgian to today), each fetching and quoting primary/official
sources (Coflein, Cadw, the Dictionary of Welsh Biography, National Trust, the Aberglasney Trust,
Wikipedia as a lead only, etc.) rather than working from memory.

**Headline findings that revise or sharpen the original brief:**

- **No dated Palaeolithic or Mesolithic site is currently recorded within the radius itself.** The
  nearest dated Ice Age evidence (Coygan Cave, Neanderthal handaxes, ~64,000–38,000 BCE) is a
  Carmarthenshire site but roughly 20 miles away, outside the radius. Within the radius, the
  earliest *attributed* (not radiocarbon-dated) monument is the Neolithic long cairn on Garn Goch —
  undated except by typology. This is very likely a survey gap rather than genuine absence of early
  activity (see Open Questions).
- **The "Battle of Llandeilo Fawr" is real and dated 1282** (16 or 17 June, sources differ by a
  day), a Welsh ambush that killed William de Valence Jr and stalled Edward I's 1282 campaign. There
  is **no evidence of a separate 1116 "Battle of Llandeilo Fawr"** — that year is instead the
  broader Gruffudd ap Rhys uprising, unlocated to Llandeilo specifically.
- **Dinefwr Castle's link to Rhodri Mawr and Hywel Dda (9th–10th century) is tradition only** —
  Wikipedia's own castle article states plainly "no archaeological remains have been dated from
  this period." The documented, archaeologically evidenced castle begins under the Lord Rhys after
  1163.
- **Carreg Cennen's popular "built by the Lord Rhys" story sits in tension with the archaeology**:
  Coflein dates the standing masonry to 1287–1321, under the English Marcher lord John Giffard,
  granted the site after the 1282–83 conquest — i.e. the castle you can visit today is substantially
  a post-conquest English fortress on a site with an earlier, less certain Welsh phase.
- **Carreg Cennen had no role in the Civil War** — it was already a ruin, deliberately slighted in
  1461–62 during the Wars of the Roses. Any framing of it as "slighted in the Civil War" is a
  conflation to avoid.
- Two real Roman forts, a probable Roman temple, and a Roman coin hoard are documented at
  **Dinefwr Park itself** — a stronger and more local Roman presence than the "Roman forts in
  Dinefwr Park" line in the brief might have suggested was speculative.
- A handful of claims are **flagged single-source or contradictory** and should not be presented as
  settled facts in-game without a further check: the Statute of Rhuddlan's exact role in creating
  Carmarthenshire; Talley Abbey's exact foundation year (1184/1185/1189) and guardianship date; the
  Vaughan family's "1531 execution" origin story for Golden Grove (contradicted by the Dictionary of
  Welsh Biography's silence on it); Newton House's 1770s remodelling and 1850s architect name; and
  several aggregator-sourced lat/lon pairs that should be re-verified against Coflein/Cadw before
  being used for precise in-game placement.

## Earliest occupation (with uncertainty)

**Wider Wales context.** The oldest human evidence anywhere in Wales is at Pontnewydd Cave,
Denbighshire — early Neanderthal remains dated to roughly 230,000 years ago [S1]. Wales was
glaciated through the last glacial maximum and became ice-free by around 18,000 years before
present (BP); people are recorded returning to Wales by about 14,500 BP, with the earliest firmly
dated evidence of (modern human) activity around 12,500 BP, at Paviland and Hoyle's Mouth (both in
south-west Wales, both well outside this radius) [S1]. The Palaeolithic conventionally ends around
11,500 BP, after which Mesolithic hunter-gatherers occupied Wales continuously [S1]. Only five
Palaeolithic sites are known in the whole of Wales, all on carboniferous limestone, and none of them
fall within 10 miles of Llandeilo [S3].

**Nearest dated Ice Age evidence to Llandeilo.** Coygan Cave, near Laugharne, produced Neanderthal
"bout coupé" handaxes dated to roughly 64,000–38,000 BCE [S2]. This is the nearest dated Palaeolithic
findspot in Carmarthenshire, but it is approximately 20 miles from Llandeilo — outside the 10-mile
radius — so it is context, not local evidence.

**Within the 10-mile radius itself: no dated Palaeolithic or Mesolithic site was found.** This may be
a genuine absence, or (more likely, given the general pattern of Welsh upland archaeology, where
flint scatters are usually found only when forestry work or peat erosion happens to expose them) a
survey gap. It should not be presented in-game as proof no one was here that early — only that no
one has yet found and dated the evidence.

**Earliest attributed evidence within the radius: the Neolithic long cairn on Garn Goch.** A 55m
stone long cairn sits on the highest summit of the Garn Goch hillfort complex (near Bethlehem,
~4.3 miles from Llandeilo). Coflein describes it only in typological terms — "its appearance
suggests a Neolithic date" — with **no excavation, no radiocarbon date, and no artefact assemblage**
tying it to a specific date. This should be treated in-game as "probably Neolithic, undated"
[S4][S5].

A nearby monument, **Waun Pwtlyn** (~5.6 miles from Llandeilo, near Llangadog), was **previously
catalogued as a Neolithic long barrow or chambered tomb**, but Coflein's current record reclassifies
it as a **natural glacial (esker) landform**, aligned with the direction the Tywi valley's glacier
flowed — while noting it nonetheless remains a scheduled ancient monument [S6]. This is a genuinely
open, honest example of archaeological reinterpretation, and a good candidate for an in-game ⓘ note
on how "monuments" can turn out not to be monuments at all.

Beyond these, the radius holds a scatter of undated ("Prehistoric," typologically Bronze-Age-style)
standing stones, burnt mounds and round cairns — the Ffairfach standing stone (the closest
prehistoric monument to Llandeilo itself, ~0.6 miles away), the Bryngwyn standing stone near Trapp,
five burnt-mound sites around Ffairfach/Llandybie/Llangadog, and five round-cairn groups around
Dyffryn Cennen/Llangadog. None of these has a firmer date than "prehistoric" from a scheduling
description [S7]. A possible cave with prehistoric human activity has also been claimed beneath
Carreg Cennen Castle, though the sourcing for this specific claim is weak and unverified [S14].

**Conclusion for the game's earliest scene:** the honest earliest-occupation content is: (1) people
were in Wales from ~14,500 BP and in south-west Wales specifically from at least the Mesolithic,
(2) no dated site within the 10-mile radius itself currently proves exactly when people first
reached this stretch of the Tywi valley, and (3) the earliest attributed local monument — the Garn
Goch long cairn — is Neolithic by appearance only, not by date. Any in-game "first people arrive"
moment for this specific 10-mile circle should carry a clear ⓘ marker acknowledging this gap, rather
than presenting a false-precision date.

## Candidate magnetic key dates for the slider (~25)

## Open questions / contradictions between sources

1. **Battle of Llandeilo Fawr date**: 16 June (two independent chronicle-citing sources) vs
   17 June 1282 (Wikipedia) — a one-day discrepancy, unresolved.
2. **No "1116 Battle of Llandeilo Fawr" was found.** The Dictionary of Welsh Biography's account of
   Gruffudd ap Rhys's 1116 rising describes a broad, unlocated guerrilla uprising, not a
   named battle at Llandeilo. Treat the 1116 date as a likely misattribution/conflation with the
   real 1282 battle.
3. **Waun Pwtlyn**: once scheduled as a Neolithic long barrow, now reclassified by Coflein as a
   natural glacial feature — yet still formally a scheduled ancient monument. Good in-game example
   of archaeology correcting itself.
4. **Dinefwr Castle's "founded by Rhodri Mawr / seat of Hywel Dda" tradition has no archaeological
   support.** The only documented fortification is post-1163, under the Lord Rhys.
5. **Carreg Cennen's "probably built by the Lord Rhys" framing (Cadw's own wording) is in tension
   with Coflein's own dating of the surviving masonry to 1287–1321**, under the English lord John
   Giffard after the conquest. Both are from reputable sources but frame the same building
   differently — worth surfacing in-game as a genuine debate rather than picking one silently.
6. **Dryslwyn's founder** is given as an unnamed "local Welsh prince" by Coflein, but as "perhaps
   Rhys Gryg" by tourism-oriented sources — not confirmed.
7. **Dryslwyn's fate under Glyndŵr in 1403** ("seized," per Wikipedia only) is single-sourced and
   sits oddly next to Dinefwr and Carreg Cennen both successfully resisting Glyndŵr in the same
   year — needs a dedicated Glyndŵr-rising academic source (e.g. R.R. Davies) to confirm or correct.
8. **Talley Abbey's foundation year** varies across sources: 1184, 1185, or 1189.
9. **Talley Abbey's state-guardianship date is contradictory** (1933 vs 1949 in different
   summaries); Cadw's own page gives no date at all. Unresolved — flagged for a Coflein site-record
   check (coflein.gov.uk/en/sites/92750 was identified but not fetched in this pass).
10. **Statute of Rhuddlan / creation of Carmarthenshire**: one Wikipedia article credits the 1284
    statute with creating Carmarthenshire; another states the 1284 statute covered only north Wales
    (Anglesey, Merionethshire, Caernarfonshire, Flintshire), and that south Wales was already
    governed via the 1240 honours of Carmarthen and Cardigan. A genuine, unresolved contradiction —
    do not present "1284" as Carmarthenshire's clean founding date without a better legal-history
    source.
11. **The Vaughan family's "1531 execution" origin story for Golden Grove** (Rhys ap Gruffydd of
    Dinefwr allegedly executed for treason, lands passing to the Vaughans) appears only in a
    non-authoritative secondary source and is **not mentioned at all** in the Dictionary of Welsh
    Biography's own Vaughan-family article. Treat as unverified, likely apocryphal, pending a better
    source.
12. **Carreg Cennen and the Civil War**: a popular framing that the castle was "slighted in the
    Civil War" is contradicted by both Cadw and Wikipedia, which agree it was already ruinous from
    the 1461–62 Wars of the Roses slighting. No Civil War-era action at the castle is documented —
    avoid repeating this conflation in-game.
13. **Newton House's 1760s–1780s turret/battlement remodelling and its 1850s architect (R.K.
    Penson)** are each single-sourced to Wikipedia; National Trust's own history page does not
    corroborate either detail.
14. **Coleg Sir Gâr's start date at Golden Grove (1952)** is single-sourced and unverified.
15. **No specific documented Rebecca Riots incident was found within the actual 10-mile Llandeilo
    radius.** The famous first attack (Efailwen, 13 May 1839) is estimated 20–30 miles away — likely
    outside the radius. Flag as an open research gap; do not invent a local riot.
16. **No dated Palaeolithic or Mesolithic site is yet recorded within the 10-mile radius itself** —
    likely a survey gap (uplands are under-surveyed) rather than genuine absence, given the wider
    Carmarthenshire pattern (e.g. flint scatters found elsewhere in Wales only via forestry work).
17. **The "30,000 attendees" figure sometimes quoted for the 1176 Cardigan Eisteddfod** is very
    likely a modern tourism-style exaggeration and has been omitted from the documented claims.
18. **Several coordinate pairs in this document are derived from OS grid references** using a local
    linear approximation (not a full OSGB36→WGS84 transformation), or come from non-Coflein
    aggregator sites. All are labelled "approximate" throughout; the load-bearing ones for the v1
    scope (Newton House, Aberglasney, Golden Grove, Dinefwr) should be re-verified directly against
    Coflein/Cadw before being used for precise in-game camera placement or terrain alignment.
19. **Mynydd Myddfai's monument cluster** (standing stones, ring cairns, round barrows) calculates
    at roughly 11–12.5 miles from Llandeilo — just outside the 10-mile/16km radius — so it is
    excluded from the main table above, but noted here in case the radius is later reconsidered.
20. **Llandovery (Llandingad)** is roughly 11–12.4 miles from Llandeilo — just outside the radius —
    so its early medieval inscribed stones are excluded from this document.
21. **Llansawel, Llanwrda, and Llandyfan** were named in the brief as possibly-in-range places but
    were not specifically researched in this pass; their distance from Llandeilo and any notable
    sites there (e.g. Llandyfan's holy well) remain open for a follow-up research pass.
22. **Ammanford is confirmed within the radius** (~7 miles straight-line / ~8 miles by road from
    Llandeilo, ~19 minutes' drive) and should be treated as "edge of radius, included."
23. The claim of prehistoric cave activity and possible Iron Age hillfort phase beneath Carreg
    Cennen [S14] came from a general aggregator page whose exact identity was not pinned down by the
    researching pass — worth re-finding and re-verifying, ideally against Coflein/Cadw directly.
