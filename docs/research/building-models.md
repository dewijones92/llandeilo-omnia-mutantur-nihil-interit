# Measured models of the important buildings, phase by phase

Research to close the gaps listed in [`../design/models.md`](../design/models.md) ("Research gaps
hit while modelling"), written 2026-10-02 from sources fetched that day. The brief is in
[`briefs/building-models.md`](briefs/building-models.md). WebSearch was used 5 times; every other
source was fetched directly (curl, read as raw text). The Caple excavation report (2007) and the Cadw
guidebooks were **not** read: no full text was found online. What they say reaches this note only
through Coflein, the Cadw listing reports and medievalheritage.eu, all of which cite them.

## How the dimensions were measured

Most numbers below are **measured from published plans**, not stated in text. The plans are the
Cadw-derived drawings reproduced on medievalheritage.eu, each with a scale bar. The method:

- Download the image, find the scale bar's pixel length, then read wall faces and pier centres along
  straight pixel profiles (a dark-pixel run is a wall).
- Where two different drawings of the same building exist (Talley, Dryslwyn), measure both and
  compare. They agree on proportions to within about 4%. Their scale bars do not always agree, so
  absolute sizes carry about **±5%** (Talley, Dinefwr, Carreg Cennen) or **±10%** (Dryslwyn, whose
  bar is only 58 pixels long).
- A measured figure is marked "measured" and is a reconstruction from a drawing, not a stated fact.
  It is cross-checked where a text gives the same figure.
- One plan's scale bar is wrong (Dinefwr S24: it would make the keep 21m across), so that plan was
  used for shape only.

Confidence labels: **high** = cross-checked by two independent sources, or a measurement that a
text confirms; **medium** = single source, or measured from one drawing; **low** = inferred;
**gap** = not found.

## Summary

- **Dryslwyn's three wards are all Welsh work, built before the 1287 siege.** Coflein says the
  castle "was twice expanded before, in 1287, it was captured" [S1]. The Cadw listing report puts the
  middle ward under Maredudd ap Rhys (1233-71) and the outer ward under Rhys ap Maredudd after 1271,
  "perhaps after the war of 1282-3" [S6]. Only the walled borough may be English (contested). So the
  current model (all three wards from 1225) is wrong for the middle ward before about 1240-70 and for
  the outer ward before about 1271-87.
- **Talley's built church was about 49m long, not 73m**, with a four-bay nave and **only a south
  aisle**: the north wall of the finished nave stands on the line of the north arcade [S13][S14][S16][S17].
  The western four bays and the planned north aisle never rose above footings.
- **Dinefwr's great tower stands on the east side of the inner ward**, just inside the east
  curtain; the small round tower is at the north-west angle [S22][S23].
- **Carreg Cennen's gatehouse is in the north curtain** [S25][S26][S27]. The barbican runs east
  along the north side of the inner ward, then turns south down into the eastern outer ward [S26][S27].
- **The old Llandeilo bridge was standing by 1577**, not first built about 1700 as the app has it
  [S35][S36]. Whether it was restored or replaced after the floods of 1795-98 is contested.
- **St Teilo's tower height was not found.** The Victorian church reuses the medieval foundations
  (one source), so its ground plan is the medieval one [S30].

## Talley Abbey

Coflein NPRN 92750, **SN 63281 32772**, on the surviving tower [S10]. The app point (263199,
232800) is about 85m west-north-west, as the review found.

### What was built

The church was designed as an aisled, eight-bay cruciform church about 73m long [S12][S19]; 235ft
(71.6m) [S16]. It was finished in a shorter form "probably in the earlier thirteenth century" [S10].
"Only the eastern four bays of the nave and south aisle were actually built higher than foundation
level, and of the planned north aisle, only the easternmost bay was built as a small room accessible
from the north transept" [S17]; the same in [S12][S18]. The finished church "was 162 feet long,
including a four-bay nave with only one aisle, to the south" [S16]. On both Cadw-derived plans the
finished nave's north wall (early 13th century) is built along the line of the north arcade, and a
plain west front closes the fourth bay [S13][S14].

### After 1536: the parish church

- Dissolved 1536 [S10][S12]. "The choir and presbystery were retained to serve as a parish church
  after 1536 and were not completely forsaken until 1772-3" [S10]; "only the chancel ... until 1772"
  [S12]; "the church was converted for use by the parish" [S16].
- **Contradiction:** Coflein's St Michael's record says "The original church comprised the abbey
  church itself, and was noted to be 150ft in length in 1710" [S11]. 150ft is 45.7m, close to the
  whole finished church (about 49m), not the choir and presbytery alone (about 26m from the west
  crossing piers).
- Post-Dissolution work is drawn on both plans: a platform against the north wall at the nave's east
  end [S13][S14], which S16 calls "a platform along the north wall of the nave, installed into the
  converted parish church", and a blocking in the south aisle wall [S14].
- St Michael's was built in 1772-3 from abbey stone [S10][S11]; "In the eighteenth century the
  church is thought to have had a double-gabled roof" [S11] (this is St Michael's, not the abbey).
  Part of the abbey's walls collapsed in 1845 [S12]. Clearance began before 1906 and continued from
  1933 [S10].

## What this means for the current plans (for the owner)

These are observations against `src/content/buildings.ts`, not changes made.

- **Dryslwyn:** split the model into three phases: inner ward only (about 1225 to the mid-13th
  century), with the middle ward (mid-13th century to about 1271-83), with the outer ward and the
  southern apartment block and chapel tower (about 1283-87 onward). The outer gatehouse belongs at
  the outer ward's north end, the middle gate in the middle ward's east wall.
- **Talley:** the plan's nave (both aisles, 52m) should become a four-bay nave with a south aisle and
  a solid north wall, about 22m long, plus footings of four more bays and the north aisle. The tower
  footprint is nearer 11.5-12m than 9m. The surviving walls `['n','e']` match the sources.
- **Dinefwr:** the great tower belongs on the east side, the small tower at the north-west angle.
- **Llandeilo Bridge:** the seven-arched bridge should start by 1577, not 1700.
- **Newton House:** steep turret roofs from 1857 until 1934.

## Open questions

- St Teilo's tower height. Welsh Government LiDAR (1m surface model, OGL) would answer it, and also
  the 1848 bridge's width, Newton House's footprint and the standing heights at Talley and Dinefwr.
  It is not exposed on the DataMapWales WCS (checked: only noise maps are), so a tile download is
  needed.
- Talley: did the parish church use only the choir and presbytery, or the whole shortened church
  (150ft in 1710)? What were the cloister ranges' widths and heights?
- Dryslwyn: who built the middle ward, Maredudd ap Rhys or Rhys ap Maredudd? Was the borough wall
  Welsh (1280s) or English (after 1287)? Caple (2007) would settle both.
- Dinefwr keep diameter: 12m [S21], 13.5m [S22] or about 14m over the base [S23].
- Llandeilo old bridge: restored seven arches or a new three-arched bridge after 1798? Its arch
  spans and its exact line downstream of the present bridge.
- Heights almost everywhere (curtains, gatehouses, ranges) are still not in any source read.
- The Cadw guidebooks (Rees and Caple 2007 for Dinefwr and Dryslwyn; Platt and Robinson / Cadw 2007
  for Talley; Lewis 2006 for Carreg Cennen) and Caple's report were not read directly.
