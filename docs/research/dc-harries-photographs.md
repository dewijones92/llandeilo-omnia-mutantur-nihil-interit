# D. C. Harries's photographs of Llandeilo

Research and gathering for [GitHub issue #1](https://github.com/dewijones92/llandeilo-omnia-mutantur-nihil-interit/issues/1)
(agreed by Dewi, 2026-10-09; Claude's own choices here are under the proactive mandate,
[ADR 0027](../adr/0027-claude-proposes-and-builds-under-a-proactive-mandate.md)). This is the
research stage only: the in-app photo layer is built later. Dewi's instruction for this item
(2026-10-09): "ignore licensing problems, just credit the person", so every photo carries a credit and a
catalogue id and URL, and no licence check blocks it ([ADR 0026](../adr/0026-open-licences-including-nc.md)).
Written 2026-10-09 from pages fetched that day; the images were downloaded and each one looked at.

## Summary

- **31 photographs credited to D. C. Harries were found and downloaded**, all on People's Collection
  Wales (PCW), uploaded by the Hengwrt community account for the **Llandeilo Fawr Town Council** collection
  [S4][S5]. Three further items tagged to him (a man and horses near Llandeilo, c. 1910) are not
  views of the town and were not downloaded [S5].
- **None of the 31 is an NLW catalogue item.** The National Library of Wales holds his glass negatives
  (bequeathed 1976) [S1][S2], but the only part of that collection it has put online is the First World War
  military portraits (about 930 negatives catalogued, 200 digitised) [S3][S6]. No NLW record, shelfmark or
  online image of a Llandeilo street, building or the station was found, and the NLW's own collection page
  names no catalogue record for the volumes of prints it says it holds [S1]. So each photo is credited to
  Harries and to the Town Council collection, not to the NLW, and whether these prints were made from the NLW's
  negatives is not stated anywhere read.
- **The station: yes, two views, but of c. 1910 and c. 1918, not c. 1900.** A platform view (c. 1910) and a
  crowded platform view (c. 1918). A third item, captioned as the station (c. 1910), is **not the station**:
  the image shows a colliery screening plant with wagons lettered "Pantyffynnon Best Anthracite Coal"
  (*miscaptioned*; see below).
- **Streets: yes**: Rhosmaen Street (seven photographs), Bridge Street, King Street, New Road, Crescent Road,
  George Street (Abbeyfield) and Carmarthen Road (the White Hart), plus the Market Hall on Carmarthen Street.
  Most are captioned c. 1900.
- **Placement:** 28 of 31 are placed on the OS grid, 14 as *documented* (the caption names a building or
  junction whose position a source gives) and 14 as *reconstructed* (the street is named but the plot or the
  camera position is not known); accuracy ranges from 25 m to 500 m (one, the Ffairfach butter factory).
  3 are not placed (a farmhouse not named, a street "possibly Llandeilo", and the miscaptioned colliery).
- **Dates are the uploader's captions**, "c. 1900", "c. 1910", "c. 1920", "c. 1928". They are not Harries's own
  and no independent dating was found, apart from the Post Office, whose 1897 inscription is on a postcard
  of the same building [S15]. The app's year range for "c." is plus or minus 5 years (a convention of this note, not a source).

## Who he was

**Documented** (three sources, two of them independent of each other):

- D. C. Harries was a Llandeilo photographer; he "started his business in about 1888" and "died in 1940, aged 75";
  his collection was "bequeathed to the National Library of Wales in 1976 by Hugh Newton Harries, the last
  surviving son" [S1]. Wikipedia gives the same death and age, and "approximately 1888" for his start, citing
  *Kelly's Directory* (1891) and his obituary in the *Carmarthen Journal*, 8 March 1940 [S2]. The NLW's catalogue
  record gives his dates as **1865 to 1940** [S3].
- The "C" was invented, "to distinguish him from others of the same name locally"; he was David Harries [S2][S6].
- He worked from studios in Llandeilo and Ammanford, later also Llandovery [S1][S2]. The NLW's exhibition page
  gives **48 Rhosmaen Street, Llandeilo** and **Quay Street, Ammanford** [S6], and its catalogue record gives the
  creator as "D C Harries, Rhosmaen Street, Llandilo" [S3]. Photo 2282411 shows a hanging sign reading
  "D. C. HARRIES" on a Rhosmaen Street building.
- He lived by studio portraits and family occasions, and photographed farm machinery, mining at Ammanford,
  limestone quarrying at Llandybie, the Glanbran mansion (dismantled 1930) and the Llandeilo to Ammanford bus
  accident [S1][S2]. The accident is the photographs 2282141, 2282156 and 2282171 here.

**Where the sources disagree** (not settled; shown as stated):

| Point | One source | Another |
|---|---|---|
| Start of the business | "about 1888" [S1]; "approximately 1888" [S2] | "from the early twentieth century" [S6] |
| Size of the collection | "2,500 negatives were selected, printed and bound into volumes" [S1]; "930+ glass negatives" of military portraits alone [S3] | "800 glass negatives" donated [S2]; "around 800 studio portraits" of servicemen [S6] |
| Name of the donor's collection | Harries [S1][S2][S3] | Coflein calls a source "the D.K. Harries collection at the NLW" [S18]; probably a slip for D. C. |

## Where the collection is and how it is catalogued

- **National Library of Wales** (Aberystwyth). Collection page "The D C Harries Collection": 2,500
  negatives "selected, printed and bound into volumes of about 100 prints each", on open shelves in the
  Reading Room; no shelfmarks and no catalogue links [S1].
- **NLW Archives and Manuscripts**, one file only: "D C Harries Collection: Military Portraits", reference
  code *D C Harries Military Portraits*, [c1890-c1947], "930+ glass negatives", 165 x 120 mm or smaller,
  Virtua system control number vtls006307433; "Access to the original work by authorised permission only";
  digital images "available at https://crowd.library.wales/en/s/dcharries"; "Copyright NLW" [S3]. That
  crowd.library.wales address presented a certificate for another host when fetched on 2026-10-09 (a
  certificate error), so it could not be browsed from here. **A person with browser access should look
  there, and at the NLW's main catalogue (discover.library.wales), for any street views the NLW has
  digitised.**
- **People's Collection Wales**, the "Llandeilo Fawr Town Council Collection" (collection 2287136, 40
  items, owner Cyngor Tref Llandeilo Fawr Town Council, uploaded by Hengwrt, items added 1 September 2025) [S4].
  Its tag "d c harries" (tag 251856) lists 34 items [S5]. The tag the issue named, **tag 8118, is
  "carmarthenshire"** (a county-wide tag with hundreds of unrelated items), not a Harries tag [S5]. Every
  item page says "Content can be downloaded for non-commercial purposes" under PCW's Creative Archive
  Licence, with the Creator field "D C Harries".
- NLW's collection page does not say the PCW prints come from its negatives; the PCW items name only the
  Town Council as owner.

### Captions that do not hold up

- **2282096, "Llandilo Railway Station, c. 1910", is not the station.** It shows coal wagons lettered
  "PARK AND BLAINA COLLIERIES, PANTYFFYNNON BEST ANTHRACITE COAL" and a timber screening plant still being built.
  The wagon lettering says whose coal it was, not where the plant stood, so it is not placed. The data file marks
  it `miscaptioned` so the app cannot show it as the station by accident.
- **2282381** is captioned "possibly Llandeilo"; not placed.
- **2281961**: the item text says "possibly" by Harries, though PCW credits him.
- **2281901's** title omits Harries but the item's Creator field and tag name him.

## How the placements were made

1. **Streets:** OSM street geometries (Overpass, fetched 2026-10-09) converted to EPSG:27700 with GDAL [S13],
   cross-checked against OS Open Map Local's roads [S12].
2. **Buildings:** Cadw's listed-building coordinates for The Cawdor Arms Hotel (262922, 222364) and No. 1
   George Street / Abbeyfield House (262773, 222288) via British Listed Buildings [S10][S11]; Coflein's grid
   references for Llandeilo Bridge (SN 62757 22001) [S7], the Provisions Market (SN 62760 22450) [S8] and
   Llandeilo Railway Station (SN 63340 22580) [S9]; OS Open Map Local for the Church of St Teilo, the post
   office and the railway station symbol [S12]; OSM for Salem chapel, Capel Newydd, the Civic Hall, the White Hart
   Inn and the Victoria Hotel's substation and "Yr Hen Vic" [S13].
3. **The 1890s map:** the National Library of Scotland's OS 25-inch England and Wales tiles (1892 to 1914)
   [S14] were stitched at zoom 18 and overlaid with 100 m grid crosses; the grid crosses land on the
   map's street pattern (for example Carmarthen Street and Rhosmaen Street), and every placed point was drawn on
   the map and looked at. It also showed that the road north from Railway Terrace is labelled **"Station Road"**
   (so the Farmers' Co-operative photo is on what is now Rhosmaen Street north of the town), and that the old
   "P.O." stood about 40 m west of today's post office.
4. Nothing was placed from memory. Where no source fixed a plot, the point is the middle of the named street
   and the accuracy says so.

  (263357, 222603), within about 15 m of the track on the 1890s map (the line passes E 263279 at N 222361), not at a
  station building. The map marks the station building at the Coflein position. Not changed here (out of this task's scope); it is listed in
  [open-questions](open-questions.md) for a decision, since the train model's stopping point may depend on it.

## Gaps and leads not followed

- **No NLW-held street or station views were found.** Possible routes: the NLW's catalogue (discover.library.wales)
  and the crowd.library.wales site [S3], the printed volumes on the Reading Room shelves [S1], R. Iestyn Hughes,
  *D. C. Harries: A Collection of Photographs* (1996), cited by Wikipedia [S2], and the *Carmarthen Journal*
  obituary of 8 March 1940. None was read.
- **Other photographers' c. 1900 views of the station and streets exist on PCW but are not Harries**: two
  postcards of Llandeilo station c. 1900 by A. W. Williams of Llandeilo (items 2084711, 2084726) [S16]; dozens of
  Williams postcards of Rhosmaen Street, Bridge Street, King Street and New Road (c. 1904 to 1913) in the
  collection of Ieuan Lewis; NLW-owned photographs of civilians and soldiers on the station platform in
  1914 to 1918, creator unknown (items 408896, 408900) [S17]. They would answer "the station around 1900"
  better than the Harries set does, and need an owner decision before being gathered, since the credit
  would be to someone else.
- **The Coflein record for Taliaris (archive 6194830)** copies a c. 1910 photograph from the NLW's Harries
  collection ("D.K. Harries") [S18]; it shows the NLW holds country-house views as well, which are not yet found online.
- **Not found:** the exact plots of R. Griffiths's garage, D. Hughes's shop, the Victoria Hotel, Whitecliffe House,
  Central Garages and the Ffairfach factory; the occasion of the arches in 2282426 (the shields read A and R); the
  date of the bus accident beyond "c. 1928".
- The "48 Rhosmaen Street" address [S6] could fix the studio's plot if Rhosmaen Street's numbering were known;
  it was not found, and the Cawdor (no. 72) sits near the south end.
